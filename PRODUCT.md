# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Customs brokers, and operations staff at NVOCCs and freight forwarders, filing ISFs and entries with US Customs. They work at a desk during the business day, usually on a large monitor, moving between MyCargoLens, email and carrier portals. Their job on any screen is to find what is late or about to be, fix it, and send it.

## Product Purpose

MyCargoLens files ISFs and customs entries with CBP and shows where every shipment stands. Success means no missed ISF cutoffs, few rejections, and a team that can see its whole workload without asking anyone.

## Brand Personality

Calm, exact, trustworthy. Plain words, stated once. The interface reads as serious software made by people who know customs work, not as a demo.

## Anti-references

- Anything that looks AI-generated: bento card grids, gradient heroes, progress rings, glow, rows of equal KPI tiles with sparklines and green arrows, numbers that count up, uppercase eyebrow labels, entrance animations that stagger in.
- Plain, unstyled lists that show data without helping anyone act on it.

## Design Principles

1. Useful before beautiful, then both. Every figure on a page earns its place by the decision it helps someone make.
2. Say only what is stored. An ISF that has gone out is "Sent", not "with CBP"; never claim "cleared" or "released" without a stored response that says so. An estimated date says "Est.".
3. One vocabulary across the app. The same colours mean the same things on every page (urgent, soon, info, entry, done), and the same words name the same states.
4. Motion shows a change of state and nothing else. Short, interruptible, and off when the system asks for reduced motion.
5. Flat and human. Real tables with column headers, thin borders, no decoration that a person would not have drawn on purpose.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: text contrast at least 4.5:1, including on tinted backgrounds. Every action reachable by keyboard with a visible focus ring. Reduced motion honoured everywhere. Status is never shown by colour alone; a word always goes with it.
